## 2. Research Findings

### 2.1 YouTube Policies (Official Sources)

**Reused Content Policy:**
Channels that repurpose existing content without substantial original commentary, modification, or educational/entertainment value are ineligible for monetization. Reaction and commentary videos are NOT automatically disqualified — the determining factor is whether meaningful original value is added. No policy changes in 2025-2026.
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392*

**Inauthentic Content Policy (July 15, 2025):**
Renamed from "Repetitious Content." Targets mass-produced, template-generated content with little variation. Explicitly does NOT change rules for reaction/commentary channels that add genuine value. Key risk: if our videos look too templated across uploads.
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251*

**YPP Requirements (2026):**
| Tier | Subscribers | Additional Requirement | Unlocks |
|------|------------|----------------------|---------|
| Expanded | 500 | 3 uploads in 90 days + 3,000 watch hrs OR 3M Shorts views | Memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks |
| Standard | 1,000 | 4,000 watch hrs (12 months) OR 10M Shorts views | Full ad revenue sharing |

Note: Shorts watch hours do NOT count toward the 4,000-hour long-form threshold.
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851, answer/13429240*

**Content ID — How It Works for Gaming:**
- Video gameplay footage is NOT eligible as a Content ID reference (only game publishers can submit)
- Music tracks in games DO trigger automated claims from music rights holders
- "Track" policy (what we received) = rights holder monitors but does NOT monetize or block. We keep our ad revenue
- This is confirmed by our test uploads
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/2605065, answer/6013276*

**AI Disclosure:**
- Must disclose AI-generated content that appears realistic during upload
- Failure to disclose: content removal or YPP suspension
- Our cartoon peanut character is clearly non-realistic, making compliance straightforward
- YouTube may add labels even without creator disclosure if AI detected
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491*

**Shorts Monetization:**
- Creators keep 45% of allocated Shorts ad revenue
- Typical Shorts RPM: $0.01-$0.06 per 1,000 views (50-100x less than long-form)
- Primary value is DISCOVERY, not revenue: 74% of Shorts views come from non-subscribers
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220*

### 2.4 Legal Landscape

**US Fair Use — Reaction Content:**
Landmark case: *Hosseinzadeh v. Klein (2017)* — h3h3 reaction video ruled fair use under "criticism and comment." Factors favoring us: transformative purpose (AI character commentary), criticism/analysis focus. Factors against: amount of original used (full videos in compilations).

**CRITICAL NEW RISK — Cordova v. Huneault (2026, N.D. Cal.):**
Court ruled that downloading YouTube videos using stream-ripping tools violates DMCA Section 1201(a) by circumventing YouTube's "rolling cipher" access controls — EVEN IF the resulting video would qualify as fair use. This directly applies to yt-dlp usage.
*Source: torrentfreak.com, blog.ericgoldman.org*

**Implication:** Our use of yt-dlp/4K Video Downloader carries independent legal risk beyond copyright fair use analysis. Mitigation: use screen recording instead, or acknowledge risk is theoretical (no enforcement against small channels observed).

**UK Fair Dealing:**
Narrower than US fair use — requires genuinely for "criticism or review," proportionate amounts only, sufficient acknowledgement. Unlike US, this is a closed list, not a flexible test.
*Source: Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, Section 30(1)*

**Content ID Dispute Risk:**
Dispute -> claimant has 30 days -> rejection -> appeal -> claimant has 7 days -> can issue DMCA takedown (converting claim to STRIKE). Rule: NEVER dispute music-only claims that don't restrict.
*Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/7000961*

**AI Commentary — Untested Territory:**
No court has ruled on whether AI-generated commentary qualifies as "transformative" under fair use. YouTube's policy focuses on "meaningful human editorial judgment" regardless of AI involvement.

## 7. Legal Risk Assessment

### 7.1 Risk Levels

| Risk | Probability | Severity | Overall | Mitigation |
|------|------------|----------|---------|------------|
| Music Content ID claims | Very High (100%) | Very Low (non-restrictive) | LOW | Accept claims, don't dispute |
| Video Content ID claims | Low (not observed) | Medium (revenue redirect) | LOW | Memberships as backup |
| DMCA copyright strike | Very Low | Very High (channel termination at 3) | MEDIUM | Never dispute, credit creators |
| "Reused Content" demonetization | Medium | High (lose all ad revenue) | MEDIUM-HIGH | Substantial commentary, vary format |
| "Inauthentic Content" flag | Low-Medium | High (channel penalty) | MEDIUM | Vary templates, human review |
| yt-dlp DMCA 1201 risk | Low (no enforcement pattern) | Medium (statutory damages) | LOW-MEDIUM | See Section 7.2 |
| Sidemen direct takedown | Very Low (tolerance pattern) | Very High | LOW | Credit, don't compete directly |

### 7.2 Cordova v. Huneault (2026) — Download Tool Risk
A 2026 California court ruled that stream-ripping tools violate DMCA Section 1201(a) by circumventing YouTube's access controls, even when the resulting video qualifies as fair use. This directly applies to yt-dlp and similar tools.

**Practical assessment:**
- No enforcement has been observed against individual small creators
- The case targeted a commercial stream-ripping service, not end users
- Risk is theoretical but legally real
- Mitigation options: (a) use screen recording, (b) accept the risk given no enforcement pattern, (c) obtain content through other means

### 7.3 Fair Use Strength Assessment
| Factor | Our Position | Strength |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Purpose (transformative) | AI character adds commentary, analysis, humor | Moderate-Strong |
| Nature of original | Published entertainment content | Neutral |
| Amount used | Full episodes in compilations | Weak |
| Market effect | Different use case (background viewing vs original) | Moderate |

**Overall fair use position: MODERATE.** We are stronger than passive re-uploads but weaker than heavily transformative works (like h3h3's format). The full-episode usage in compilations is our weakest factor.

**Strongest defense:** Content ID "track" policy means the rights holders themselves have chosen not to restrict — this is implicit tolerance.
